Getting started

This browser version offers a camera mirror, fullscreen and independent quadrant adjustments. You can save and reload your view and camera preference. Optional sessions record participant details and pain scores before, during and after use.

Start with a comfortable view

  1. Place your computer or camera on a stable surface, with enough light to see yourself. Adjust the physical camera to include the area you want to view.
  2. Choose Start camera and allow the browser to use the camera. Microphone access is not requested.
  3. If the wrong camera opens, scroll inside Camera & view, choose another in the Camera list and press Use selected camera.
  4. Raise a hand to check the starting view. If you need the opposite direction, press Reverse view. Pressing it again returns to the previous direction. A loaded setup restores its saved choice.
  5. Choose Stop when finished. Closing or leaving this page also releases this page's camera stream.

Use a larger view

Choose Fullscreen in the top toolbar. It works on your current screen; a second monitor is not required. The settings panel hides so the image has more room. Use Show controls to adjust your view, and Hide controls when finished. The settings panel scrolls independently while the video stays visible. On a narrow screen the video stays above the controls, in both normal and fullscreen views.

The toolbar stays visible, including Stop camera. Press Escape or the exit button to return to the normal page. Leaving fullscreen does not stop the camera or change your orientation or setup. The previous panel visibility is restored.

If your browser cannot enter fullscreen, the view expands inside the browser window instead. Use Exit expanded view or Escape to return. The whole camera frame is fitted without stretching or cropping, so borders may remain. Fullscreen does not increase camera resolution or reveal more of the scene.

Fullscreen always starts with a button press; loading a setup will not enter it automatically.

Find the camera and session controls

Camera & view stays on the right of the live image. Its settings scroll while the camera stays in place. Hide controls gives the image more space.

Choose Create session in the top toolbar to open a focused session window. The participant name, limb-loss sides and descriptions, and starting pain score are shown there. On smaller screens this window fills the screen. Close or Escape returns to the camera and preserves your entries.

After Start session, the window closes and the top button becomes Current session. Use it to review the fixed participant details, see recent scores, save JSON or end the session. Record pain opens a small scoring panel beside the camera; number-key scoring also works without opening the session window.

Use a separate camera window

Choose Open camera window in the Camera & view heading to put the live mirrored view in its own window. The main window keeps camera settings on the right and the Create session / Current session button at the top.

Arrange the windows side by side on one screen or move the camera window to another monitor. Both windows share one camera and session. Number-key pain scoring works in either window; the camera window also provides Record pain, Stop camera, End session and Fullscreen in a compact toolbar. Hide controls leaves a Show controls button so you can restore the toolbar. Downloads are requested by the main window, so check its Downloads/save prompt after a final score.

Return to main window in the camera window, or Return camera here in the main window, brings the view back. Closing the camera window does the same and leaves the session available. Keep the main window open: it owns the camera and session records. Closing it closes the camera window too and can lose undownloaded records.

If the browser blocks the new window, allow pop-ups for this site or keep using the embedded view. Your browser may open it as a tab; you can move it to a separate window using your browser's controls. Open this view with the app's button rather than bookmarking the camera-only page, which has no independent camera or session.

Choose the side you want to show a mirrored limb

Use the side with the limb you want to copy as the source. Show its reflection on the side with the missing or affected limb.

  • Right arm affected: copy the left arm into the region showing the right arm.
  • Left arm affected: copy the right arm into the region showing the left arm.
  • Use the same approach for the legs.

In ARM, select the receiving quadrant—the area you want to change, then turn on Left/right mirror. Leave the source region unchanged. Selecting the source region instead could hide the limb you intended to copy.

The quadrant names describe positions on the screen, which may differ from the person's anatomical right and left. Before making a selection, have the person move the limb you want to copy and identify it in the preview. Then select the corresponding region on the opposite side.

Check that the original limb remains visible and its mirrored copy appears where intended. Reset alignment only centers the dividing lines; turn off a quadrant's mirror toggle to undo its mirror choice.

Choose mirroring and align the panes

Scroll inside Camera & view to Quadrants. The camera stays on screen while the controls scroll. Select a receiving quadrant by its position on the screen. Turn on Left/right mirror to copy the reflected opposite side in the same row; turn it off for the normal image. Each quadrant remembers its choice. There is no top/bottom flip.

The two sliders under Shared alignment affect all four panes, regardless of which quadrant is selected:

  • Horizontal mirror center moves the vertical dividing line left/right.
  • Vertical mirror center moves the horizontal dividing line up/down.
  • Reset alignment returns both lines to 50%, without changing any left/right mirror choices.

Values are percentages from the left or top of the full image. The range is 10–90% so every pane retains some space. Panes remain joined with no gaps. The dividers reveal more or less of complete camera images. Moving them never enlarges, shrinks or moves those underlying images. A person remains the same size even when the panes are unequal. Borders around the entire video can still appear to fit your screen.

You can focus a slider and use arrow keys. With all mirror toggles off, moving the boundaries does not change the normal camera image. Turn on a quadrant's mirror choice to see its reveal boundary move. The reflection stays centered on the full image; the dividing line only controls which version is visible. Reverse view changes the base camera direction before quadrant processing.

Sources always come from the original camera image. Check posture and physical camera position each time; saved settings cannot move the camera for you.

Save your setup for another day

The shared Participant name in Create session / Current session is optional and is saved in both camera-setup and session JSON files. If entered, it begins both filenames. Without a participant name, filenames begin with the UTC date and time. The optional Setup label describes the view and is saved separately; it is not the participant name. Choose Save setup, then keep the JSON file in a folder you can find again. Your browser may save it to Downloads or ask where to put it.

Next time, choose Load setup and select that file. This restores your mirror direction, all four left/right choices, the shared mirror center, participant name and preferred camera name. It does not start or switch the camera automatically. Confirm the camera choice and press Start camera or Use selected camera.

Older setups keep their camera direction and any left/right choices. Earlier top/bottom flips and individual offsets are removed, and shared alignment starts centered. An import message explains this. Older setup labels are kept as labels, not treated as participant names. New camera setups use version 4 and need build 10 or later. Loading a different name during an active or completed session keeps that session's name and explains why. Choose New session before loading another participant's setup.

A setup cannot move the physical camera or restore your posture. Check your position each time. Camera names can change or be shared by two devices, so always check the preview.

Only setups saved by this browser version are supported here. Desktop ARM study-session files will be rejected with an explanation.

Record an optional pain session

You can use the camera without a session. To record one, choose Create session in the top toolbar. A family member, clinician or the person using ARM can operate these controls.

  1. Enter a participant name or label in the session window if wanted. Both JSON files include that name and begin their filenames with it; without a name they begin with the date and time.
  2. Under Limb-loss locations and sides (optional), select all locations that apply, using the person's own right and left. For bilateral limb loss, select both sides. Add a level or description if useful. These selections do not change the mirror quadrants.
  3. Choose the pain before use score, then press Start session. Participant details are fixed for that record and summarized beneath the score buttons. Choose New session afterward to edit details again.
  4. During use, select a score whenever you want to record one. A single selection records the score immediately with its time and the current mirror settings.
  5. Press End session, then choose the final score. That selection ends the record and requests a JSON download. Continue session returns to during-use scoring if you are not finished.

Keyboard: keys 1–9 record scores 1–9; the 0 key records 10. For an actual pain score of zero, choose No pain (0) in the panel. Number shortcuts pause while typing in text fields or using sliders/select controls. Holding a key does not repeat measurements.

The toolbar shows the session state while controls are hidden. Its Record pain button opens a small scoring panel. End session opens the session window to ask for the final score. In the separate camera window, End session accepts the final score through Record pain or the number keys. Stopping the camera does not end the session, and ending the session does not stop the camera.

Save session JSON can download progress at any time or request the completed file again. Check Downloads or the browser save dialog. New session starts a fresh record with a new ID; check the retained participant details and choose a new starting score. Camera setup files and pain-session records are different files.

Records remain only in the current tab until downloaded. Closing, reloading or leaving the page can lose unsaved entries. There is no automatic upload or background saving. Session files contain the entered participant information, so keep them in a location you choose.

If the camera does not open

  • Permission denied: check the site's camera permission in your browser and the browser's permission in your computer's privacy settings. Then press Start camera again.
  • Camera busy: close other applications using the camera and try again.
  • Camera missing: check its connection, choose Default camera, and retry.
  • Restricted computer: an administrator may need to allow the site or camera. This app cannot bypass those restrictions.
  • Still waiting: look for a browser permission prompt. You can press Stop even while that prompt is open; a late permission response will not start the preview.
  • Changes do not appear: stop the camera and reload the page. The build label at the bottom helps identify which version is running. When checking startup orientation, start before loading a setup, since the setup restores its own flip choice.

Where information goes

The camera image is processed on this device. This version has no video recording, accounts, analytics, or upload service. Saving a setup creates a local JSON download containing the setup name, camera preference, mirror choice, quadrant choices, shared alignment, and save date. Saving a session creates a separate JSON file containing participant details, limb-loss selections, timestamps, pain entries and mirror settings. Loading reads the file on your device.

A hosting provider may log ordinary website requests, such as your IP address. The app does not send it camera images or setup files. A file saved to a cloud-synced folder may be synced by that folder's service.

About this version

ARM is an experimental visual-feedback tool. These instructions explain its controls; they are not a treatment plan or a promise of pain relief.
